\section*{Summary}
\texttt{v04\_tuned} keeps \texttt{v01\_catan\_fixes}' one-move lookahead and replaces its hand-set
weights with weights \textbf{found by an evolution strategy against our whole opponent pool}. On
1{,}500 fresh local games it beats \texttt{v01} against every opponent and wins
\textbf{61.0\% $\pm$ 5.5} head to head against \texttt{v01}. The requested target was 70\% against
F. \textbf{We do not expect 70\% against F}: our estimate is about 57--58\% on the organisers'
judge. Section~\ref{sec:expect} explains why, and Section~\ref{sec:next} what would be needed to
get closer.

\section{What is different from v01}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Every weight is tunable.} F's 12 value-function weights (VP weight fixed at
    $3\times10^{14}$) and v01's 4 Catan fixes (2:1 port, 3:1 port, road progress, army progress)
    are read from \texttt{weights.json} beside \texttt{agent.py}. If the file is missing or broken
    the agent falls back to v01's weights.
  \item \textbf{Two new relative features} that let the tuner react to the opponent:
    \texttt{road\_race} $=$ (our longest road $-$ theirs), clipped to $\pm5$, once theirs is
    $\ge 4$; and \texttt{opp\_vps} $=$ a penalty per opponent public victory point, so taking
    Longest Road or Largest Army \emph{from} them counts twice.
  \item \textbf{The weights were tuned} with \texttt{tools/tune.py} (new, in the repo):
    \begin{itemize}
      \item $(1+\lambda)$ evolution strategy, $\lambda=10$ mutants per generation; each mutant
        multiplies 1--3 weights by $e^{\mathcal{N}(0,\,0.7)}$ (log-scale steps, signs kept).
      \item Fitness $=$ mean win rate against F, \texttt{road\_army\_f}, \texttt{card\_hoarder} and
        \texttt{v01}; 40 games per opponent per candidate, seats alternated.
      \item All candidates of a generation play the \emph{same} fresh seeds (paired comparison).
        The best mutant must beat the incumbent there \emph{and} on a second fresh seed block
        before it is accepted (protects against picking a lucky mutant).
      \item Seeds 200000--200840 only (never the 100000 quick-check or 990000 judge seeds).
        45 minutes on 11 cores: 11 generations, about 13{,}000 games, 9 accepted steps.
        Every candidate is logged in \texttt{tools/tune\_log.csv}.
    \end{itemize}
  \item Nothing else changes: same search (one move ahead, one sampled world), same CPU as v01
    (about 200\,ms per game; 0 errors, 0 games over budget in local runs).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{What the tuner changed}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrl}
\toprule
Weight & v01 & v04 & Reading \\
\midrule
\texttt{enemy\_production} & $-1.0\times10^{8}$ & $-2.6\times10^{9}$ & $\times 26$: deny the opponent's income \\
\texttt{discard\_penalty} & $-5$ & $-27$ & avoid holding $>7$ cards \\
\texttt{reachable\_production\_0} & $1.0\times10^{4}$ & $2.4\times10^{4}$ & value spots we can build now \\
\texttt{army\_size} & 10.1 & 17.4 & slightly more for knights \\
\texttt{num\_tiles} & 1 & 2.2 & tie-breaker \\
\texttt{hand\_resources} & 1 & 0.49 & tie-breaker \\
\texttt{port2} & $3.0\times10^{7}$ & $1.4\times10^{7}$ & ports over-valued \\
\texttt{port3} & $1.0\times10^{7}$ & $4.6\times10^{6}$ & ports over-valued \\
\texttt{opp\_vps} & (new) $3\times10^{13}$ & $1.1\times10^{13}$ & per opponent VP \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The one large change is \texttt{enemy\_production}. With it, the agent puts the robber on the
opponent's best hexes and takes building spots the opponent needs much more aggressively. In a
two-player game with no trading between players, every resource the opponent does not collect is
a resource they cannot replace; F's weight on it was 26 times too small for this setting.

by the tuner), same seeds for both agents. ci95 $\approx \pm5.5$ points.

\section{Expected win rate, and why}\label{sec:expect}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Test (organisers' kit) & v01 measured & v04 expected \\
\midrule
Judge vs F (1{,}000 games, seed 990000) & 55.3\% & \textbf{57--58\%} (range 54--61) \\
Head to head vs v01 (400 games) & --- & \textbf{57--61\%} \\
\texttt{road\_army\_f} / \texttt{card\_hoarder} / \texttt{greedy\_builder} & 48.8 / 71.3 / 79.5\% & $+2$ to $+5$ each \\
Official, vs O (Hyde's bot) & 39.3\% & 41--47\% (guess)  \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Why only about $+2$--3 against F.} Locally, v01 scores 58.0\% against F but 55.3\% on
    the organisers' judge: our local harness reads about 3 points high (it hands the agent the real
    game instead of a sample with re-drawn hidden cards, and the engine is not the organisers'
    patched build). We assume the same offset for v04, so its 60.3\% maps to about 57--58\%.
  \item \textbf{Why we expect more against stronger opponents (v01, O).} The gain is largest where the
    opponent is good at building: against v01, a stronger F-like player, v04 wins 61\%. Denying
    income hurts an opponent that would otherwise out-produce us. O beats F, so we expect part of
    this to carry over, but we have no way to measure O before uploading.
  \item \textbf{Why not 70\% against F.} (1) The agent still looks only one move ahead, like F. The
    two agents differ only in \emph{how they score} positions, and Catan has a lot of dice luck,
    so even a clearly better scorer moves the win rate by points, not tens of points: every
    change measured so far (v01, v02, v03, v04) moved it by 0 to 6 points against F. (2) The tuning was short: 11 generations, each candidate judged on 160 games
    ($\pm$4 points of noise), so only steps of about 3+ points could be detected. (3) More search
    on the same value function did not help (v03: 55.6\%, no change), so search alone will not
    close the gap.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Strength score.} Strength $=0.5\cdot\text{win}_F + 0.5\cdot\text{win}_O$. v01 scored
$0.5(0.541)+0.5(0.393)=0.467$. With the estimates above, v04 would score about
$0.5(0.57)+0.5(0.44)\approx 0.50$.

\section{What would be needed to approach 70\%}\label{sec:next}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{More tuning} (cheapest): continue from \texttt{weights.json} on the team machine,
    with \texttt{tools/tune.py}, \texttt{--start agents/v04\_tuned/weights.json},
    \texttt{--seed0 300000}, \texttt{--minutes 40}. More generations and more games
    per candidate find the smaller steps the first run could not see.
  \item \textbf{Combine with exact chance expectation} (\texttt{v05\_expect\_relative}) once that is
    measured, then re-tune the combination.
  \item \textbf{A learned value function} (Jainam's \texttt{vj01} experiment): the only idea with
    10+ points of upside, but also the riskiest.
\end{enumerate}
